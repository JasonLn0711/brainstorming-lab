# Taiwan AI startup personnel cost: 7M TWD per year

Date: 2026-05-21
State: observation
Related idea: Product-constrained PhD engineering research OS
Planning bridge: not needed unless this changes current capacity or company-work interpretation

## Core Judgment

For a Taiwan AI-engineering-driven startup, annual personnel cost of NTD 7 million is low to lower-middle, not high.

The exact interpretation depends on team size and talent composition, but as a realistic operating budget it is closer to an early-stage, compressed-burn setup than a mature AI product team.

## Cost Reality

NTD 7 million per year is about NTD 580,000 per month.

This is not fully disposable salary. It includes company-side people cost such as labor insurance, health insurance, pension contribution, bonuses, and other employment overhead.

Rough market intuition:

- Experienced AI engineer: about NTD 70,000 to 150,000+ per month.
- Junior engineer: about NTD 40,000 to 60,000 per month.
- PM, backend, frontend, DevOps, and operations roles also consume meaningful cost.
- Company-side insurance, pension, equipment, annual bonus, and office overhead further reduce the effective salary pool.

## If The Company Is Truly Building An AI Product

NTD 7 million usually supports only a small team:

- roughly 3 to 5 people;
- limited salary headroom;
- founders may need to take low salary or no salary;
- scope must stay narrow.

Example monthly cost structure:

| Role | Estimated monthly cost |
| --- | --- |
| AI engineer x2 | NTD 200,000 |
| Full-stack engineer x1 | NTD 80,000 |
| PM / Ops x1 | NTD 70,000 |
| Insurance and miscellaneous overhead | NTD 50,000 to 100,000 |

This quickly approaches NTD 500,000 to 600,000 per month.

## Product Types Where 7M Is Easily Not Enough

If the company is building any of the following, NTD 7 million can become tight very quickly:

- LLM agents;
- medical AI;
- computer vision;
- ASR;
- multi-agent systems;
- SaaS platforms.

The expensive part of an AI company is often not GPU cost. It is people.

Especially costly capabilities include:

- independent architecture ownership;
- deployment ability;
- MLOps;
- data pipeline construction;
- customer requirement translation;
- product boundary management.

In Taiwan, engineers who can build end-to-end AI systems are not cheap.

## When 7M Can Be Normal

NTD 7 million can still be typical for an early-stage Taiwan AI startup if the company:

- heavily uses open-source models;
- relies on founders doing large parts of the engineering work;
- uses students or lab support;
- is connected to professor labs;
- receives government project funding;
- works through industry-academia collaboration;
- is still doing demos or PoCs rather than commercial scaling.

Many Taiwan AI startups are structurally closer to:

- lab extensions;
- AI consulting shops;
- PoC-driven teams;
- government-project-driven teams;
- SI companies with AI components.

For this type of company, the first two years can run with very compressed people cost.

## The Better Question

The key question is not whether NTD 7 million is "a lot."

The better question is:

> Where does the 7 million go?

Two companies with the same annual personnel cost can have very different survival odds.

## Pattern A: Dangerous

- 10 low-paid engineers.
- No architecture owner.
- Everyone is firefighting.
- The team keeps changing demos.
- Product direction is unclear.

This structure can burn the budget without producing a durable product.

## Pattern B: Healthier

- 2 to 4 strong people.
- Small and clear scope.
- A specific commercial problem.
- Founder can sell.
- Technical and product boundaries are explicit.

This structure may survive even with a smaller budget.

## Deeper Startup Lesson

An AI company is usually not just a "model company."

It is also:

- a talent-intensive company;
- a problem-definition company;
- a systems-integration company;
- a commercial-deployment company.

Many teams do not die because the AI model is weak. They die because:

- scope is too large;
- cashflow is insufficient;
- people burn rate is too high;
- demos never become products;
- founders do not understand product boundaries;
- the company accepts too many custom projects.

This is a common failure mode for Taiwan AI startups.

## Research / PhD Interpretation

This observation is relevant to the product-constrained PhD engineering research line.

If a PhD student works inside or near this kind of AI startup environment, the training value should not be judged only by the apparent technical stack. The deeper training question is whether the work exposes:

- real constraints;
- product boundary decisions;
- human capital bottlenecks;
- architecture ownership gaps;
- deployment and maintenance pressure;
- demo-to-product failure modes;
- commercial problem definition.

Company-shaped work becomes doctoral training only if these pressures are abstracted into reusable research questions, evaluation criteria, design rationale, or system failure patterns.

## Smallest Next Test

When evaluating a Taiwan AI startup or lab-company project, estimate:

1. annual personnel cost;
2. number of people supported;
3. number of people who can independently own architecture;
4. whether the company is PoC-driven, consulting-driven, product-driven, or research-driven;
5. whether current work is producing reusable artifacts or only demos.

If the budget is around NTD 7 million, assume severe scope discipline is required unless there is strong founder labor, lab subsidy, government funding, or unusually efficient talent.

## 2026-07-22 Extension: Market Scale, Productization, and Value Capture

The personnel-cost question sits inside a larger system. A Taiwan AI company can have enough cash for a small team and still lack a scalable business when its effective market is fragmented, each deployment requires substantial customization, or customer relationships and pricing power belong elsewhere.

A stronger diagnostic now uses seven linked conditions:

1. effective market;
2. product repeatability;
3. global distribution;
4. delivery capacity and total implementation cost;
5. growth capital;
6. talent density and career value;
7. value capture through pricing, IP, data, customer relationships, and recurring revenue.

This reframes the earlier cost observation. Low salaries and talent loss can be downstream effects of a project-based, low-margin model rather than isolated labor-market problems. Export access expands demand, while productization and value capture determine whether that demand funds better talent, durable R&D, and continued expansion.

The strongest Taiwan position is likely to use local manufacturing, semiconductor, medical, security, and enterprise settings as demanding validation environments and lighthouse-customer sources, then package the resulting domain knowledge into repeatable products for global niches. The smallest next test remains company-specific: estimate all seven conditions for one Taiwan B2B software or AI firm and identify the binding constraint.
